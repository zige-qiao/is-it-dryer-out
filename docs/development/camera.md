# Camera capture and diagnostics

**Audience:** Camera maintainers

**Status:** Current main reference; appearance and uniform glass sheets on the separate `dark-mode` branch remain Unreleased where labelled

**Owns:** Permissions, capture lifecycle, framing, zoom, lighting and standalone diagnostics

**Reviewed:** 14 October 2026

[Documentation index](../index.md) · [User guide](../user-guide.md)

Capture owns camera resources and settled framing. Recognition owns pixel analysis and its worker budget; see [Camera recognition](camera-recognition.md). The production camera and the standalone diagnostic have separate settings and lifecycle requirements.

The camera loader is shared by the sheet camera entry and Indoor summary camera shortcut, with controller wiring owned by `app.js`.

The entry button shows a reduced-motion-aware busy spinner during loading; failures restore it and show an inline status above the manual controls. Retry clears failed loading state. Before native import, parallel resource checks warm the HTTP cache and keep ordinary download failures out of the browser module map, which can remember failed imports for the page session. A graph-coverage test maintains this camera-only resource list; creation of the recognition worker stays deferred. Controller initialisation rolls back listeners and label observers on failure, and repeated successful initialisation does not bind them again.

Camera modules use the controller/environment-injection pattern. capture.js owns session-scoped readiness, camera controls and the injected preview canvas. The muted playsinline video remains in the document as an invisible decoding source. The 4:3 canvas paints the centred source crop at no more than 30 fps, capped at 1280 pixels on its longest edge; capture reuses the stored displayed bounds at higher resolution. Displayed geometry and control revisions gate capture, including after zoom, resize or orientation changes. Frame callbacks have a timer fallback; stop clears rendering, callbacks, canvas pixels and late constraint updates. Canvas failure offers camera recovery.

Startup conceals the preview for at least 500ms after a valid frame and requires three geometry samples unchanged for 250ms. The independent 1.5-second reveal deadline and five-second no-frame watchdog remain. Actual iPhone rendering and responsiveness require device verification. Zoom uses the settled track setting as its baseline, native settings readback and a residual digital crop for the 1×, 3× and 5× buttons. zoom-presets.js owns accessible session selection, independently of transient track startup. Selection updates immediately, remains available during switching and coalesces to the latest request; capture stays gated through track and framing settlement. Presets are disabled during capture and reset to 1× on retake.

Native zoom steps are respected. device-session.js identifies an explicit telephoto label after permission and selects its exact device ID at 3× and 5×, verifying readback; 1× restores the starting camera. The telephoto baseline maps to displayed 3× and residual crop remains shared with still capture. Switches serialise/coalesce with zoom and lighting, release late streams, recover the previous camera on failure, and preserve session lighting decisions. The displayed ratio does not guarantee optical magnification or physical lens locking. Native controls serialise/coalesce to ten updates per second and preserve flash settings. help.js owns capture/review tooltip interactions; changing views switches guidance and closes help.

The controller updates status kind and text together: processing/info, ready only for valid drafts without a pending box, attention, and distinct error. Ready capture status is hidden; startup/switching/settling/problem messages appear top-centre. The capture message is “Capturing…” for frame capture or “Hold still — taking photo…” for still capture. Presets are a bottom-centred overlay pill with flash at bottom-right. A separate temporary canvas retains the preceding preview through track replacement; CSS applies 6px blur to presentation canvases only until current framing renders. stop releases both canvases and the switching class. The inner guide and dimming overlay are removed.

Camera review uses overscroll-behaviour-y: none on its scroll container to suppress local elastic bounce while preserving overflow scrolling. This scope excludes manual entry, capture and other sheets. The crop editor draws unassigned boxes directly and supports pointer/touch movement, corner resizing and keyboard adjustments. Completed changes re-read only the affected region; taps/cancels preserve drafts. Unit symbols assign new boxes, with explicit assignment/discard fallback. The labelled Retake pill is excluded from crop pointer handling. Drafts never change app state before confirmation. saveIndoorReadings('photo') preserves the stored JSON shape and sets temporary source metadata.

The saved useStillPhotos and showVoiceButton booleans default false; showCameraButton defaults true. Entry visibility is independent of active camera/voice controls and support checks. The Settings camera-options group follows showCameraButton through the shared preference update; hiding it preserves capture preferences and returns any nested focus to the parent switch. Camera-help navigation focuses Auto flash when visible, otherwise Show camera button. Shared focus restoration skips hidden openers. Camera-frame mode draws directly from the decoding video using stored settled bounds; it skips ImageCapture, blob decoding and torch preparation. Still mode retains the bounded actual-photo path with torch preparation/restoration.

Mode selection is fixed at shutter time and never silently falls back. The controller retains one private native-resolution framed canvas capped at 4096 pixels without upscaling, independently of the displayed 1600-pixel photo. It releases that canvas on retake, dismissal, confirmation or backgrounding.

The standalone `diagnostics/camera-flash-test.html` / `diagnostics/camera-flash-test.js` diagnostic lives in `diagnostics/`. Serve it over HTTPS for phone camera testing. Start the rear-camera feed, then compare default `ImageCapture.takePhoto()` with `takePhoto({ fillLightMode: 'flash' })`. Both attempts remain enabled when the method exists, even if no flash mode is advertised or the capability query fails. Torch controls use the track's independent capability; Pause freezes playback without stopping the track. Each photo attempt switches active torch off first (failure prevents capture), displays the full returned photo and requested mode or exact error, then resumes/reopens the preview and restores torch and pause choices.

Timed-out torch/capture owners are stopped before another attempt. It does not use recognition, video-frame capture, zoom, cropping, or saved app readings. Capability, control and capture waits are bounded; leaving or backgrounding releases the camera and photo. Confirm actual flash firing and preview recovery on the phone: a returned Blob alone does not prove either.

The diagnostic's Copy log button copies a bounded, timestamped event log with browser identity, safe track settings, advertised photo capabilities, requested capture modes, returned image size/type/dimensions, errors, and torch/preview recovery. If clipboard access fails, it selects the read-only log for manual copying. Logs remain in page memory across camera stops/backgrounding, and contain no photos, object URLs, device/group IDs or camera labels. Actual flash firing must still be reported by the tester.

Auto flash defaults on only in this standalone diagnostic. Every 500ms it samples fresh preview pixels from the central half into a 32×32 canvas; three consecutive average luminance readings below the provisional 50/255 threshold latch a flash decision for that camera session. It enables supported torch and Auto photo requests flash after that decision, retaining torch isolation/restoration during capture. Sampling pauses during playback pause, camera operations or backgrounding and ignores missing/unchanged frames. Manual torch control disables Auto flash for the page session; switching Auto flash off releases torch it activated. Detection or automatic torch failures disable the option with an explanation and leave manual controls available.

Copy log includes brightness, thresholds and decisions, without sampled pixels. Restarting the camera clears the decision; reloading restores the default-on option. Tune the threshold from actual iPhone observations before considering a main-app setting.

The diagnostic's zoom control switches to a separately exposed telephoto candidate at **3× or above**, returning to the starting rear camera below 3×. It recognises an explicit telephoto device label as a hint, with a camera selector for manual assignment; it never infers lens type from enumeration order. Selection requires an exact device ID and settings readback, but physical lens identity still needs the phone's lens-cover check. If no candidate is identified, it keeps the starting camera and reports this. It applies supported native zoom only, with no software crop; selected telephoto starts at its native baseline at the 3× threshold, so the displayed request is not a verified optical ratio.

Switches serialise with capture/lighting, retain the low-light latch and pause/torch choices where supported, and recover the preceding camera on failure. Reopening after capture uses the selected camera and restores native zoom. Selected-camera opening and zoom have bounded waits; late streams are released. Logs include lens roles, native settings, requested zoom and failures, excluding device IDs and labels. This experiment changes no production lens-selection behaviour.

Production Auto flash is a saved default-on preference. Fresh central-half 32×32 preview samples every 500ms latch low light after three luminance readings below 50/255; sampling pauses while camera controls, capture, Settings or backgrounding suspend it. Manual flash overrides Auto for the session. New camera sessions reset the latch; lens switches retain it. Automatic failures leave manual capture available. Camera help is an interactive region linking to the setting.
