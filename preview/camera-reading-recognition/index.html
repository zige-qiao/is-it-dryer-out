Glossary

AH — Absolute humidity: The amount of water vapour in the air, measured in grams per cubic metre (g/m³). Lower outdoor AH means outdoor air contains less moisture per cubic metre than indoor air.

RH — Relative humidity: How close air is to saturation at its current temperature, shown as a percentage. Warming air lowers its RH without removing water vapour.

DP — Dew point: The temperature at which air becomes saturated when cooled. Condensation can form on surfaces at or below this temperature.

ACH — Air changes per hour: The hourly volume of incoming air divided by the room volume. For example, 2 ACH means airflow equivalent to twice the room’s volume each hour; it does not mean all the original air is replaced.

Let in: The estimated RH of outdoor air once it reaches your current indoor temperature.

m³ — Cubic metres: A measure of room volume: length × width × height in metres.

How estimates work
  • Timing is approximate and depends on room volume, opening setup, forecast wind, and the indoor-outdoor temperature difference.
  • Future outlooks use the current indoor readings; they do not predict how the room will change before that hour.
  • The line shows outdoor absolute humidity against the indoor dashed reference. Bars show estimated airflow.
  • ACH means air changes per hour: an estimated ventilation rate, not a measured airflow.
  • Real results vary with airflow, room layout, and indoor moisture sources.
